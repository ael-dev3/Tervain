## Movement, collision, and camera

Previously, endpoint-style movement and broad obstacle footprints could leave fast motion, corners, upper surfaces, and camera clearance inconsistent. The candidate uses shared finite scenery bounds and continuous contact checks:

- Walking, evasive steps, attack lunges, recoil, and actor-contact pushes sweep the player's body against circles and oriented boxes, including rounded box corners, before sliding the remaining displacement along a contact. Actor contact handling is a player/world correction; it does not rework NPC models or behavior.
- Terrain steps are checked along the movement path. Authored doorway thresholds and lighthouse treads supply standing surfaces; support-only geometry supplies feet/ceiling support without acting as an infinite horizontal wall. Poses and footsteps use actual movement, so blocked input does not animate continued travel.
- Standing checks use the player's body height at the selected floor. Saves, respawns, and graphics rebuilds preserve a valid stair/gallery or doorway height instead of dropping that position to the terrain below. Arbitrary saved air heights, nonfinite values, and obstructed placements fall back to safe supported ground. Save format remains 1.
- The orbit camera checks its continuous boom against finite-height scenery and terrain with near-plane clearance. It retracts immediately at obstruction, recovers gradually after clearance, and resets follow state on spawn/load/teleport/respawn. Low objects are not treated as infinitely tall walls. Reduced Motion suppresses the bounded impact recoil.
- Interaction selection combines distance, view/facing, and finite line of sight. Only an explicitly hosting object is excluded where necessary; intervening scenery still blocks the interaction. Observation remains nonblocking and does not confer meeting/trust/quest effects.

Implementation: [colliders](../../../src/world/colliders.ts), [player](../../../src/presentation/player.ts), [supported placement](../../../src/world/playerPlacement.ts), [camera boom checks](../../../src/presentation/cameraObstruction.ts), [camera rig](../../../src/presentation/cameraRig.ts), and [interaction selection](../../../src/presentation/interactionTarget.ts).

These are kinematic controllers and authored support surfaces. Root motion, foot IK, general rigid-body simulation, and swimming are not added.

## Desktop records and input

The HUD uses a bronze compass, health/stamina display, a ten-slot item hotbar, and a time/purse readout. Its center slots are labeled `1–0` and start empty; inventory/journal/map remain separate keyboard actions. Journal, inventory, and map use parchment with native controls and visible focus. Weapon equipment and consumption are real game commands; mana, active spells, training, and new quest functions are not introduced.

- Journal tabs separate observed, reported, concluded, and other existing records with roving focus and arrow/Home/End navigation. Inventory category filters and grid navigation expose actual item details, quantities, equip/unequip and consume actions, and quick-slot assignment. Healing is disabled when it cannot act.
- The map shows known places and a player marker whose heading follows the same yaw convention as the world. Unknown place names are not exposed; a caption and legend explain the map's marks. Scroll zoom, drag pan, keyboard controls, and a personal pin work as described below.
- Panels stop simulation, release pointer capture, clear gameplay action buffers, and restore focus through updates and nested return. Held keyboard/gamepad actions must be released or neutralized before they can trigger again in play. World look/zoom ignores wheel and pointer input inside panels.
- Blur and hidden-tab transitions pause without accumulating gameplay time. Quickload resets transient player/camera state while restoring a valid standing position. Graphics rebuilds clear buffered input and retain the existing player action/transform when that placement remains safe, together with the menu's ships and score-led grove state.
- If initial world creation or a graphics rebuild fails, play stays paused behind an accessible Retry action; disposed scenery cannot resume updating and underlying controls stay inert. Retry creates the world again without double disposal or adding another frame loop.
- Text scale, high contrast, Reduced Effects, Reduced Motion, remapping, and hold/toggle choices remain. Desktop readability and keyboard/mouse acceptance are the current target; physical-controller verification is still separate evidence.

## Inventory, hotbar, map, and world pickups

### Interaction reference

Reviewed the [official Gothic 3 English manual](https://store.steampowered.com/manual/39500/) on 1 October 2026. Printed pp. 14–19 describe inventory categories, hover details, click-to-equip/consume, equipment highlighting, drag/drop quick slots activated with `1–0`, spellbook assignment, and collected documents/maps. Printed pp. 31–33 describe restorative food/potions and regional navigation maps. Tervain adapts these interaction patterns with original assets. Its **E** pickup key and finite line-of-sight targeting are our implementation, not a key claim from this manual. Personal map pins are our addition. No Gothic or Blizzard models, textures, icons, or other assets are copied.

### Implemented candidate behavior

- **Assignment and activation:** drag a carried weapon/remedy from inventory into a slot, or select it and click a slot/press `1–0`. Drag existing bindings to swap them; keyboard-accessible Move left/Move right also swap adjacent slots. Clear removes a binding, and Delete/Backspace clears the focused editor slot. Assignment changes a shortcut, not item ownership or stack count. Each item has one binding; assigning it elsewhere moves that binding. In play, `1–0` or a usable HUD slot equips the weapon or consumes its remedy. Spells remain a future scope, not fabricated current bindings.
- **Equipment and consumption:** inventory offers Equip/Unequip for the owned rusted sword and Use for restorative food/remedies. Carrying or picking up the sword no longer automatically equips it for a fresh game. Full health refuses consumption without spending the item; valid use consumes one and clamps restoration to maximum health. Passive tools, quest items, clothing, materials, and learned-skill records are not presented as active hotbar actions.
- **Persistent state:** new games have ten null slots, null weapon equipment, and no personal map pin. Format-1 saves retain slots, equipment, marker, stacks, and taken-pickup state. Legacy saves equip an owned sword only when `equippedWeapon` was absent; explicit null stays unarmed. Invalid/unknown or duplicate bindings are sanitized. A depleted binding remains grey with zero carried, can be moved/cleared, and never supplies an item or usable stack.
- **Map navigation:** scroll zooms about the pointer, drag pans, and a click places one personal pin. Arrows pan, `+`/`−` zoom, Enter/Space pins the center, and Home fits the map. Controls can center on the player or a known place and clear the pin. Pan/zoom are retained when reopening the panel during the app session; the personal pin is saved across load. Viewport pan/zoom are not currently serialized across page reload. Pinning records a note only: it does not discover a place, reveal an unknown label, move the player, or enable fast travel.
- **World items:** 32 new grounded, original placements supply shore apples, bread, healing herbs, field mushrooms, and iron scrap, alongside the four existing sword/brace/wrench/cache pickups. They are sparse provisions, plants, and salvage along the coast, forest trail, inland waystation, keeper compound, Rillford, and quarry. E takes a reachable, visible item through the same finite reach/facing/line-of-sight selection as other interactions. Taking it adds its real stack and durably hides that placement; repeated pickup/save/load cannot grant it again. There is no respawn or additional quest-tool giveaway. Iron scrap is carried salvage; a crafting system is not added.

The 32 new loose-item placements contribute **11,316 construction triangles in five instanced batches**. These are source geometry/resource counts, not a visible-frame count or measured GPU cost.
