## Camera, audio, and runtime corrections in 0.0.5

The follow camera avoids canonical tree trunks, while leaves remain nonblocking. It queries obstruction candidates once for the whole camera boom, pulls in immediately, and eases back out after clearance. Flora and forest-floor instance selection skips unchanged camera views; camera movement, turns, and projection changes still refresh visibility. These changes reduce avoidable work and obstruction, without establishing a hardware frame-rate result.

Procedural ambience uses a six-second overlapping noise loop with DC removed and reserved peak headroom. Four voices share the buffer at distinct offsets and rates. Positive surf envelopes follow sea proximity; muted startup stays muted, settings are clamped, and device initialization failure cannot interrupt play. Audio-clock automation updates at most ten times per second, skips unchanged targets, and replaces pending ramps. Hidden or suspended contexts do not accumulate updates, and sources/nodes have idempotent teardown. No new recording, score, or synthesized contact/UI cue is included. Signal and scheduling tests do not replace subjective listening or end-to-end device-latency measurements.

## Water presentation follow-up, 30 September 2026

**Historical `0.0.4` review:** this water implementation is retained in `0.0.5`; the checks below do not validate the new forest opening.

**Status: implemented and locally reviewed; `0.0.4`, prepared for review in unmerged PR #2, publication pending.** Under [A23](../decisions.md), the owner asked for strong visual inspiration from [Three.js Water Pro](https://www.threejswaterpro.com/). The new water uses original WebGL wave, foam, and transmission shaders, not the vendor's FFT/WebGPU product or copied assets/code. Three.js's existing MIT-licensed [Reflector addon](https://threejs.org/docs/pages/Reflector.html) provides the optional planar capture; there is no new dependency or purchase.

- Four coastal wave bands supply displacement and matching normals. Fresnel shading, sampled scene color/depth refraction, shallow caustic detail, and shore wash give clear shallows and a connected surf edge. The indexed shoreline grid joins the north/south/west ocean horizon with shared vertices; the high-quality 2 m grid matches the rendered terrain's triangle split.
- Channels and the wetland spring use nondegenerate subdivided surfaces with sampled banks and a shallow ford. The spring surface and its outlet follow the carved basin, rather than a floating fixed-height disc. Flow-dependent channel width/height remains presentation of the existing quest allocation; terrain, collision, walkability, and the durable water decisions remain unchanged.
- Medium/high quality reuses the Grade's base scene color/depth, copies them to a separate composite, then draws only water into that destination. The water never samples the target it is currently drawing into. Coastal planar reflection is 384/512 pixels with a maximum normal refresh cadence of 10/15 Hz; first capture and target invalidation can initialize immediately. It is skipped inland. Low quality or reduced effects disables capture and releases the composite/reflection targets and sampled depth attachment.
- Grade owns the private render targets. Water materials borrow their textures and detach those bindings before world cleanup; the pass restores render target, camera layers, water visibility, and renderer state after normal or failed draws. Reduced motion freezes the cosmetic wave/ripple clocks, while quest flow and static lighting remain responsive.

Typecheck, the production build, and the full 229-test suite across 24 files pass. That suite includes 16 CPU render-pass/ownership tests for sequencing, no-feedback bindings, refresh caps, world-rebuild cache invalidation, resize, fallback, error recovery, and resource release; these mocks do not validate rendered depth or refraction pixels. Local browser checks have exercised coastal views and quality/effects transitions with no warning/error logs. The [water release handoff](../production/releases/0.0.4.md#water-presentation-follow-up) records the exact browser coverage, captured views, and remaining limits. No current FPS, reference-hardware performance, physical-controller, or deployed-build claim is made.

## What you can do

The `0.0.5` player surface focuses on a connected coast, woodland, waystation, and valley.

- Begin alone on the Grey Strand, inspect the stranded hull, and follow the old inland trail.
- Walk under a dense canopy, find trail stones and roofless remains, then reach the relocated inland waystation and Rillford.
- Observe residents without opening conversation windows or choosing replies.
- Inspect environmental evidence, read accessible records as nonblocking notices, and review durable observations in the journal.
- Use the retained movement, combat, inventory, map, save/load, and physical world interactions where available.

The earlier Dry Bell logic still models investigation, support encounters, repair, allocation, witnesses, and aftermath. Its dialogue graph remains authored data and can be exercised through renderer-free scenarios. Conversation-dependent consent, training, and testimony are not offered by the current observation-only NPC interface. A complete playable resolution remains a future interface task, not an acceptance claim for this exploration pass.

## Accessibility and comfort options

Text size (80–180%), reduced motion (steadier camera, no shake, calmer ground cover, still title view and rite geometry, frozen cosmetic water clocks), high-contrast interface, reduced effects (solid surfaces and fewer soft interface highlights; water scene capture disabled), brightness for night and shade, optional guidance, captions for important sounds, independent master/music/effects/ambience/dialogue levels, look sensitivity and invert, and hold/toggle choices remain. The dialogue level is retained for future audio; no spoken conversation or choice window is introduced here. NPC and inspection notices are nonblocking. Pausing, reading a journal/map/inventory panel, or changing settings stops the game clock; looking at a person or inspecting a clue does not. While free movement continues, day/night, witnesses, and combat can still advance. Hidden tabs suspend simulation and audio without catching up their elapsed time on return.

## Save recovery

Saves live in browser local storage under `tervain:save:<slot>:cur`, with `:prev` (the previous successful write) and `:tmp` (the in-flight write). If the current save fails its checksum the game restores `prev`, then `tmp`, and says so. An unsupported newer save format is reported as incompatible rather than loaded; a different visible build number alone is not incompatibility. `0.0.5` retains format 1, and an existing `0.0.4` save keeps its prior facts/progress without being changed into the new amnesiac opening. Clearing site data removes saves. A damaged slot can be deleted from Load.

- **People and the unarmed start:** every named person on one eleven-bone skeleton with finite, normalised skin weights; stature, feet on the ground and a head of about an eighth; nothing in hand at the start, and a blade that sheathes and draws; finite bones through every pose; fists weaker than a blade and unable to parry; deterministic painted faces whose hair, beard and mouth fall where the sculpt puts them; the wreck's guidance, a single take of the sword, and older saves keeping a blade.
- **World:** the strand and headland (dry sand at the spawn, deep open sea, no flooded hollows inland, a walkable route to the lighthouse door and to the blade in the wreck, a walkable jetty), every schedule anchor, pickup, observation point, the sluice control, the maintenance lever, the result-check points and the archive shutter has somewhere to stand, every scheduled transition and quest route has a walkable path, and the archive is closed until its door or shutter opens.

**Visual checks are by eye.** `tools/shot.mjs` takes a headless screenshot of the running dev server (`node tools/shot.mjs out.png place=shore hour=15 hud=0`; extra parameters `x`, `z`, `face`, `yaw`, `pitch`, `dist`, `cam=x,y,z,tx,ty,tz`, `fov`, `hide=forest,scatter,groundcover,scenery,sea,water,actors`, `lineup=x,z` and `lod=0|1|2` for the tree lineup). Shot mode defaults to frozen cosmetic motion; add `motion=1` to opt into animation for water/motion review. `tools/cdp.mjs` drives the page over the Chrome DevTools Protocol for scripted smoke runs (boot, new game, visit every place, read draw and triangle counts, collect console errors). Both need Chrome or Edge and `npm run dev`. No automated test judges how the game looks.

**Reference-hardware performance is unverified.** The [architecture](architecture.md) sets 1080p at 60 FPS on an agreed reference machine as a hypothesis. The [0.0.5 handoff](../production/releases/0.0.5.md) and [dated local benchmark](../production/releases/0.0.5-benchmark.txt) record the current Mac production-preview run. The earlier [0.0.4 run](../production/releases/0.0.4.md) remains separate historical evidence. Neither is a matched before/after comparison or reference-machine acceptance. The debug panel (Backquote) shows median, 95th and 99th percentile frame times, draw calls, triangles and memory counters, and can run a fixed camera route (about a minute) and print a report with the device, browser and quality preset. Frame times are the intervals between animation frames, recorded uncapped (only the simulation step is clamped to 50 ms), so they are limited by the display's refresh rate and reflect a real, running game with its clock, characters and time of day, not a frozen scene. The report prints the game hour and quest phase but does not pin them, so compare runs started from the same state. Run it on a real device before quoting any number, and attach the report.

## Known limitations

- Stand-in art throughout: generated textures and assembled geometry, not authored assets. Faces are sculpted but small and unanimated; at typical camera distance they read as a jaw, brow and nose, not expressions.
- Ordinary closed houses have complete backed exterior shells, not authored playable interiors. The archive has the enterable room, supported floor, and entry thresholds described in the [0.0.4 notes](../production/releases/0.0.4.md).
- The Gothic 3 likeness remains atmospheric and structural, not a reproduction of Ardea's own buildings. A27 replaces the earlier open-heath introduction with an original dense woodland journey informed by Boring Forest. The lighthouse remains from the owner's coastal brief.
- The shoreline pads, the beach and the sea are tuned by eye in screenshots; a wading player slows in shallow water but there is no swimming and no tide.
- Only a desktop browser has been exercised, mostly through scripted runs with synthesized keyboard events plus a few real clicks. A physical controller, touch input and other browsers were not tested.
- Browser pointer capture needs a click; pressing Esc while captured pauses through the pointer-lock change rather than a key event.
- The land ends at a low, rough-heath boundary and at the sea (open water deeper than about 0.9 m blocks walking), with a message at the boundary. There are no invisible walls across plausible openings.
- Navigation is a 2 m grid, deliberately conservative near thin walls, so residents do not use the archive door.
- Only the shrine warden can witness a forced entry in play, and only by line of sight during his short patrol behind the archive each day; other residents are not modelled as witnesses. (The waterkeeper receives the report, but her routine never passes the shutter.) A sealed archive stays open until the player steps out, so nobody is locked in.
- Emergency allocations are described as lapsing unless reviewed, and the bridge callback is stored as a flag, but neither a review nor an expiry is simulated; they are narrated only.
- NPC observations and ambient remarks are single lines. No conversation window, negotiated consent, training dialogue, or aftermath conversation is exposed in `0.0.5`; those graphs remain for future work.
- The world builds synchronously at load (ground textures are generated in slices so the loading text repaints). It takes noticeably longer than the earlier prototype because textures, trees and geometry are generated rather than loaded; the time has not been measured on a range of machines.
- Dense foliage alpha/shadows, scene build time, water capture, lighthouse beam, and sky need current profiling. No measured cost ranking or reference-device result is established.

## Decision this build supports

This pass tests whether a sparse arrival, coherent forest journey, and quiet environmental observations establish the intended original fantasy setting. It does not complete the slice's negotiation acceptance scenarios. The retained story loop is renderer-free data and can still be checked through scenarios; that is a structural result, not a measured authoring cost. Historical frame-time reports do not validate the new woodland, an agreed target device, packaged desktop behavior, or a new player's comprehension. Those still need current measurements and a playtest.
