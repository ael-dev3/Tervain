# Factions and relationships

> Status: PROPOSED original faction detail. A Templar-centered high-fantasy setting with diverse regional factions is accepted direction, and **the Templars are part of the Hegemony** under owner clarification A21. Their formal name, hierarchy, doctrine, internal wings, characters, and detailed relationships below remain working proposals. Hegemony affiliation does not import Hyperion's canon or move the local campaign into Warpkeep's main war. See [vision](../vision.md) and [decisions](../decisions.md).

## How faction membership should work

A faction is an institution with resources, promises, and internal disagreements. Its members share enough interests to cooperate, not a universal personality. Local relationships can differ from the leadership's position. Improving relations with quarry workers must not automatically buy affection from every guild in the Hearth League.

The player begins without mandatory allegiance. Access grows through practical service, credible introductions, knowledge, and choices. Entry into an order or guild can create specific obligations without immediately locking the player out of all others. Some offices become incompatible because their duties conflict; those conflicts must be explained before commitment. Final membership mechanics are specified with [gameplay](../design/gameplay.md).

## Six proposed powers

### Templars: keepers of places and obligations

**Confirmed affiliation:** part of the Hegemony (A21). The proposed local functions below describe this original order's possible work; they do not define a final Hegemony hierarchy or grant every local institution the same allegiance. The owner selected the approved Hegemony emblem for the menu (A22), rather than a new invented Templar seal.

**Purpose:** Preserve the conditions that sustain inhabited landscapes and honor the presences bound to them. Their power comes from archives, sanctuary rights, waterkeeping knowledge, rites, and trusted intermediaries. They cannot maintain every channel or protect every sanctuary alone.

The order includes practical stewards, healers, interpreters, pilgrims, and armed wardens. It is not a nation occupying all forested land. Sanctuaries possess different privileges granted by different communities; some are wealthy landholders, others survive on hospitality. An institution that preaches restraint must answer for its own inherited wealth.

Three proposed tendencies overlap rather than functioning as mandatory character classes:

- **Custodians** prioritize fragile sites and caution. Their restraint can avert irreversible damage or preserve harmful arrangements while people suffer.
- **Waykeepers** work among settlements and favor negotiated adaptation. They can produce workable compromises or concede steadily until nothing remains protected.
- **Renewers** seek changes to the order's authority and distribution of knowledge. They can expose injustice or discard practices whose purposes they do not fully understand.

Internal disputes involve who may teach rites, whether villagers can dismiss an appointed waterkeeper, and whether sanctuary land may be used during famine. A player can support the order's work while opposing a particular superior. Its main danger is becoming so certain of its guardianship that it treats living communities as trespassers.

### Hearth League: communities that keep the roads fed

**Purpose:** Defend town charters, shared labor rights, markets, and the survival of working households. The League is a coalition of villages, town councils, craft associations, and agrarian cooperatives with uneven representation. It provides food, carts, credit, lodging, skilled repairs, and collective resistance to levies.

**Charter councils** favor predictable law and negotiated privileges. **Common-work assemblies** demand direct control by people performing the labor. **Merchant circles** seek reliable routes and flexible trade. A prosperous guild can exploit itinerant workers while speaking in the name of ordinary people. League autonomy is therefore a question inside its own walls as well as in disputes with nobles.

The player can find hospitality, craft instruction, and employment here, but a council may require the player to confront the consequences of disrupting trade or withholding urgently needed labor.

### Marcher Houses: protection with an inherited price

**Purpose:** Maintain estate authority, roads, military readiness, and agricultural revenue. Their legitimacy rests on inherited titles and an actual capacity to mobilize protection when a community cannot. Rival houses disagree on the limits of that authority.

**Charter lords** bind their power to documented obligations. **Revenue stewards** centralize tolls and labor to fund repairs and retainers. **Retainer captains** judge policy through security and supplies. These roles can overlap within one household. A sympathetic lord may still depend on an abusive debt system; an unpopular steward may accurately predict an approaching grain shortage.

They need the League's productivity and Templar recognition of old grants. They can protect a sanctuary from bandits while contesting its exemption from taxation. The proposed game should let the player demand enforceable obligations from a house, not merely choose between serving nobility and killing it.

### Rimeward Clans: stewards of the headwaters

**Purpose:** Preserve household autonomy, pasture access, mountain routes, and the right to decide how upland resources are used. Assemblies negotiate commitments among communities; no single clan speaks for the entire north.

**Pass stewards** emphasize tolls and safe travel. **Forge households** seek mining access and basin trade. **Herding circles** defend seasonal routes and water quality. Their disagreements cut across kinship, so a clan's traditionalist elder and ambitious smith need not be enemies in every dispute.

Clans need lower-country grain and markets, while basin settlements depend on upstream care. Their strongest argument against outsiders is that downstream entitlement often arrives without labor or payment. Their weakness is excluding families whose settlement is too recent to carry ancestral standing.

### Salt Concord: law between scarce wells

**Purpose:** Guarantee transit, sustain oasis infrastructure, and prevent disputes between oasis states from closing essential routes. The Concord is a negotiated federation, not a desert empire.

**Well courts** administer access and debts. **Caravan compacts** prioritize commercial guarantees and shelter. **Channelwright fraternities** guard technical expertise and push for investment. Citizens can disagree over whether engineering knowledge should be inherited, purchased, or publicly taught.

The Concord trades expertise, preserved goods, glass, and access to southern routes. It needs grain, timber, and metal. Its engineers can rescue a basin community while creating a dependency on parts and skills that community cannot maintain. Its religious pluralism does not erase inequalities between well owners and people who carry the water.

### Ash Witnesses: a movement that disagrees about its warning

**Purpose:** Recognize and respond to signs that inherited agreements are failing. This movement crosses regional and institutional boundaries. Some witnesses are respected archivists or relief workers; some organize migrants; others manipulate fear or argue that catastrophe must be allowed to run its course.

**Recorders** preserve testimony, including evidence authorities would rather lose. **Shelter circles** prepare communities for displacement. **Absolutists** interpret destruction as necessary renewal. These are proposed descriptive tendencies, not automatically three formal organizations. A credible witness may be wrong about a cause; a reckless preacher may possess evidence others ignored.

The movement cannot become a convenient enemy onto which every institution's failures are displaced. Violent cells may exist, but membership alone is not proof of sabotage. Their narrative function is to challenge who gets believed, how catastrophe is interpreted, and whether preparing for failure excuses abandoning repair.

## Dependencies and tensions

These are regional defaults, not fixed reputation values. Local exceptions should have names and reasons.

| Relationship | Shared need or basis for cooperation | Recurring tension |

| Templars / Hearth League | Waterworks, medicine, local testimony, maintenance labor | Site protection can prevent farming or urgent repairs; councils resist unelected oversight |
| Templars / Marcher Houses | Sanctuary protection, records of grants, arbitration | Land exemptions, armed access, and the order's right to criticize its patrons |
| Templars / Rimeward Clans | Headwater stewardship, winter shelters, rites tied to local places | Imported authority versus inherited knowledge and assembly consent |
| Templars / Salt Concord | Waterkeeping expertise and rare supplies | Whether intervention constitutes care or overreach; ownership of taught knowledge |
| Templars / Ash Witnesses | Archives, sanctuary, concern about neglected warnings | Evidence versus revelation; institutional self-defense versus accountability |
| Hearth League / Marcher Houses | Food, roads, protection, trade revenues | Levies, debt, representation, and who pays when protection fails |
| Hearth League / Rimeward Clans | Grain for metal, wool, and passage | Prices during scarcity, seasonal labor rights, and downstream claims |
| Hearth League / Salt Concord | Caravan markets, credit, channel expertise | Merchant privilege, transport costs, and dependence on specialist contractors |
| Hearth League / Ash Witnesses | Relief networks and records of overlooked damage | Alarm can empty markets; preparation can become coercion |
| Marcher Houses / Rimeward Clans | Pass security and negotiated military aid | Territorial grants that clans never recognized |
| Marcher Houses / Salt Concord | Transit treaties, metal, loans | Toll exemptions and debt that limits political freedom |
| Marcher Houses / Ash Witnesses | Warnings useful for emergency preparation | Testimony exposing abuse; authorities treating dissent as sedition |

## Positions in the proposed central conflict

A coalition of Marcher Houses seeks an emergency mandate over Alder Basin's roads, levies, and public works. The Hearth League is the principal organized resistance to that mandate becoming permanent. Their contest supplies a visible campaign axis without reducing every individual or region to one of two teams; see the [setting's conflict proposal](setting.md#the-central-struggle-emergency-protection-becoming-permanent-rule).

Templar participation gives the mandate legitimacy in communities that distrust estate titles alone. Custodians may support guards around threatened sanctuaries while opposing quarry expansion. Waykeepers may negotiate limits on requisitioning. Renewers may publish evidence that invalidates a house's claim. These are possible positions, not an alignment chart: a single sanctuary can divide over a particular prisoner or levy.

Marcher coalition members need not agree on a permanent ruler; their competition gives the player leverage and creates dangers for people relying on short-lived promises. League resistance also divides between lawful petition, withholding work, and armed action. A rebellion that burns a granary can harm the households it claims to defend. Restoring order can be valuable while the officials demanding credit remain abusive.

Rimeward Clans defend assembly authority and pass agreements; the Salt Concord protects transit and solvency; Ash Witnesses contest the evidence used to justify emergency rule. Each can support, obstruct, or remain apart from a specific policy. Their independent obligations prevent the campaign from becoming a universal two-sided reputation meter.

## The factions at Bellwether

The slice represents three institutions directly: a Templar shrine, a League-aligned village, and a quarry operating under a Marcher estate contract. The other three appear through people and material evidence, not fully staffed faction hubs. A Rimeward stoneworker, a Saltward tool and repair manual, and optional Ash Witness testimony establish connections economically. The estate steward and recorder need not become additional principal quest actors.

No faction owns every member of a profession. Quarry workers can be devout Templars; a shrine warden can belong to a local farming family. A person may lose wages under a policy they support in principle. Such cross-cutting loyalties should drive dialogue and consequences more often than abstract declarations of alignment.
